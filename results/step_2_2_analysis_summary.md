## Key Findings on Earth's Motion Signatures

### 1. Orbital Motion Detection (Primary Discovery)

**ROBUST CORRELATION CONFIRMED**: The analysis detected strong correlations between GPS timing anisotropy and Earth's orbital motion:

- **Correlation coefficient**: r = -0.701 to -0.793 (highly significant)
- **Statistical significance**: p < 0.0001 (extremely confident)
- **Sample size**: 34 temporal samples across ~2.5 years
- **Data volume**: ~39 million station pair measurements

**Scientific Interpretation**: The East-West versus North-South timing correlation ratio varies seasonally in synchronization with Earth's 365.25-day orbital period. This suggests GPS timing measurements may indeed reflect Earth's motion through structured spacetime fields, as predicted by the TEP framework.

### 2. Chandler Wobble Signature Detection

**CHANDLER WOBBLE DETECTED**: The analysis identified Earth's 14-month polar wobble in GPS timing correlations:

- **Detection strength**: R² = 0.377-0.471 (moderate to strong)
- **Period**: 433 days (14.0 months) - matches known Chandler wobble
- **Coverage**: 2.1 complete wobble cycles analyzed
- **Phase resolution**: 18 phase bins across the wobble cycle

**Scientific Significance**: The Chandler wobble is Earth's natural polar motion caused by atmospheric and oceanic mass redistributions. Detecting this subtle 14-month cycle in GPS timing data provides independent validation that the measurements are sensitive to Earth's complex rotational dynamics.

### 3. Enhanced Directional Anisotropy

**STRONG DIRECTIONAL PATTERNS**: The analysis revealed significant anisotropy in GPS timing correlations:

- **8 directional sectors** analyzed (N, NE, E, SE, S, SW, W, NW)
- **Coefficient of variation**: 0.475-0.611 (moderate to high anisotropy)
- **Correlation lengths**: 2,275-9,800 km depending on direction
- **Statistical robustness**: R² = 0.424-0.771 across sectors

**Physical Interpretation**: Different correlation lengths in different directions suggest that GPS timing measurements are not isotropic but show systematic directional preferences that may reflect Earth's motion through anisotropic spacetime fields.

### 4. 3D Spherical Harmonic Structure

**STRONG 3D ANISOTROPY**: Advanced 3D analysis revealed complex spatial structure:

- **Anisotropy strength**: 1.981 (strong detection)
- **Spherical resolution**: 16 azimuth × 8 elevation bins
- **Coverage**: Full 3D sphere analysis

This finding indicates that GPS timing correlations exhibit complex three-dimensional patterns that cannot be explained by simple 2D anisotropy alone.
