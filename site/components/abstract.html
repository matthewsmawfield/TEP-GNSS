Abstract

We present observations of distance-structured correlations in global GNSS atomic clock networks, analyzing 62.7 million station pair measurements from 392 unique stations. All inference is done on ~25–28 distance-bin means; CIs reflect bin-level uncertainty, not 62.7M nominal pairs. We attempt 40 logarithmic distance bins; bins below the minimum-count threshold are dropped, yielding Neff ≈ 25–28 bins used for analysis. These stations are sourced from the combined, overlapping networks of three independent analysis centers (CODE, IGS Combined, and ESA Final). Using phase-coherent spectral methods, we observe exponential correlation decay patterns with characteristic lengths (λ) ranging from 3,330 to 4,549 km. These patterns exhibit systematic dependencies on station elevation and geomagnetic latitude, falling within theoretical predictions for screened scalar fields coupling to atomic transition frequencies. The consistency across multiple analysis centers provides evidence against systematic artifacts.

Analysis reveals coherent network dynamics through helical motion detection, identifying the 14-month Chandler wobble (R² = 0.377–0.471: CODE 0.377, ESA 0.453, IGS 0.471, corresponding to |r| = 0.61–0.69, p < 0.01), Earth motion beat frequencies (r = 0.598–0.962), and consistent phase structures (score = 0.635–0.636) across all centers. Crucially, East-West/North-South anisotropy ratios demonstrate robust negative correlation with Earth's orbital velocity across all three analysis centers (CODE: r = -0.701, p = 3.9×10⁻⁶; ESA: r = -0.571, p = 4.2×10⁻⁴; IGS: r = -0.793, p = 2.2×10⁻⁸). Fisher's meta-analysis yields combined p = 2.7×10⁻¹⁴ (unadjusted, df=6) or 1.4×10⁻¹⁰ (dependence-adjusted for shared network stations) with low between-center heterogeneity (Q = 1.85, I² = 0%, p = 0.39). Because centers share stations, standard Fisher p is anti-conservative; we report both values and note Q and I² (≈0%) support consistent effects under block-jackknife validation. The anisotropy metric is defined as the ratio of East-West to North-South correlation lengths (λEW/λNS) from 8-sector directional analysis. These correlations are consistent with velocity-dependent spacetime coupling as predicted by TEP. The analysis shows a primary correlation length of 3,330–4,549 km, primary pooled fits: R² = 0.92–0.97 (bin means); sensitivity subsets 0.70–0.91, and is validated by null tests destroying the signal by 15–42×, confirming the correlations are not artifacts. The findings are robust across centers and geographies.

A detailed diurnal analysis of 72.4 million hourly records over 2.5 years (2023-2025) reveals systematic temporal variations. All three analysis centers exhibit synchronized diurnal patterns with early morning coherence peaks (hours 3-10 local time), seasonal modulation showing maximum effects during spring equinox periods (seasonal-peak day/night ratios: CODE 1.292, IGS 1.184, ESA 1.074), and cross-center consistency in temporal dynamics (CV of diurnal patterns = 13.7-19.6%). Global-mean day/night ratios are more moderate (CODE 1.019, IGS 1.076, ESA 1.060). The observed patterns show time rate variations of ~10⁻¹⁷-10⁻¹⁶ fractional amplitude, with signal magnitudes following a 2× hierarchy (ESA Final > IGS Combined > CODE) suggesting processing-dependent sensitivity. Winter patterns show strong early morning acceleration phases, summer exhibits temporal stability phases, and autumn demonstrates coherent transition dynamics, with characteristics consistent with theoretical predictions for variable time flow rates.

Systematic analysis of 21 planetary astronomical events (2023-2025) reveals 8 Bonferroni-significant detections (σ > 3.0) across analysis centers. Key detections include the Venus inferior conjunction of August 2023 (5.44σ, p = 5.3×10⁻⁸, IGS Combined), the Saturn opposition of September 2024 (5.98σ, p = 2.3×10⁻⁹, IGS Combined, with 2.71σ ESA Final confirmation), the Mars opposition of January 2025 (4.47σ, p = 7.8×10⁻⁶, IGS Combined), and a dual-center confirmation for the March 2025 Mercury conjunction (4.28σ IGS Combined + 3.78σ ESA Final). Extensive validation provides substantial evidence of signal authenticity. Our findings are supported by a suite of tests, including:

  • Statistical Validation: Null hypothesis testing shows a 15-42x signal enhancement over randomized controls, and the results survive comprehensive multiple comparison corrections across 172 statistical tests with 69-85% maintaining significance after ultra-stringent corrections.
  • Cross-Validation: The signal remains robust under jackknife cross-validation (CV of λ across subsets = 3.5-6.5%, Neff = 25-28 distance bins) across all analysis centers.
  • Systematic Bias Control: The signal is shown to be independent of network geometry, distance distribution bias, and common-mode contamination. Methodological artifacts are quantified and shown to be 19.1x smaller than the observed signal.
  • Geophysical Independence: We demonstrate that the correlations are not attributable to ionospheric effects (bounded to <5% of signal strength), classical tidal forces, or other known geographic or atmospheric phenomena.
  • Gravitational and Temporal Coupling: The signal's coupling with Earth's orbital motion, planetary conjunctions, diurnal cycles, and gravitational field geometry (Moon-Chandler modulation) is demonstrated.

Standard GNSS processing systematically suppresses these signals, indicating our observations represent a conservative lower bound on the true effect size.

These observations test the predictions of the Temporal Equivalence Principle (TEP) theoretical framework, which predicted exponential correlation decay patterns with characteristic length scales in global atomic clock networks prior to data analysis. The GNSS results provide evidence consistent with networks serving as sensitive detectors of continental-scale phenomena affecting atomic transition frequencies. The multi-center consistency, systematic validation, and agreement with theoretical predictions provide substantial evidence that warrants comprehensive independent investigation across global precision timing infrastructure to determine the underlying physical mechanisms and potential implications for our understanding of spacetime structure. Raw data validation and independent replication by orthogonal datasets remain essential for confirming these extraordinary findings.
