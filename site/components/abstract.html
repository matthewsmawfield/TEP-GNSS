Abstract

We present systematic observations of distance-structured correlations in global GNSS atomic clock networks through analysis of 62.8 million station pair measurements from 367 unique ground stations across three independent analysis centers (CODE: 345, IGS: 316, ESA: 289). Using phase-coherent spectral methods (10-500 µHz), we observe exponential correlation decay patterns with characteristic lengths λ = 3,397–4,566 km (CODE: 4,563 ± 26 km [LOSO: 4,560 ± 26], ESA: 3,397 ± 44 km [LOSO: 3,397 ± 44], IGS: 3,815 ± 24 km [LOSO: 3,824 ± 24]) that fall within theoretical predictions (1,000–10,000 km) for screened scalar fields coupling to atomic transition frequencies. The multi-center consistency (coefficient of variation: 12.3%) provides evidence against systematic artifacts.

Analysis reveals coherent network dynamics through helical motion detection, identifying the 14-month Chandler wobble (r = 0.635–0.844, p < 0.01), Earth motion beat frequencies (r = 0.598–0.962), and consistent phase structures (score = 0.635–0.636) across all centers. Crucially, East-West/North-South anisotropy ratios demonstrate robust negative correlation with Earth's orbital velocity across all three analysis centers (CODE: r = -0.701, p = 3.9×10⁻⁶; ESA: r = -0.765, p = 8.1×10⁻⁷; IGS: r = -0.796, p = 8.5×10⁻⁸), with a meta-analytic combined significance exceeding 7σ (p < 10⁻¹²). This represents strong empirical evidence for velocity-dependent coupling through dynamical time fields.

Systematic analysis of 21 planetary astronomical events (2023-2025) reveals 11 significant coherence modulations (σ > 3.0) that survive Bonferroni correction. Key detections include the Venus inferior conjunction of August 2023 (5.44σ, p = 5.3×10⁻⁸, IGS), the Saturn opposition of September 2024 (5.98σ, p = 2.3×10⁻⁹, IGS, with 2.71σ ESA confirmation), the Mars opposition of January 2025 (4.47σ, p = 7.8×10⁻⁶, IGS), and a dual-center confirmation for the March 2025 Mercury conjunction (4.28σ IGS + 3.78σ ESA). Mass-scaled enhancement factors exhibit an inverse relationship with planetary mass (r = -0.85), with Mercury showing ~110,000× mass-normalized coupling versus Jupiter's ~5×, challenging simple gravitational models and suggesting resonance or near-field mechanisms. The temporal clustering of significant signals during the March 2025 dual conjunction (Mercury + Venus within 8 days) provides potential evidence for multi-planet interference effects.

Comprehensive validation provides compelling evidence of signal authenticity through: (1) Distribution-neutral validation demonstrating 99.4% signal preservation under equal-count binning (R² = 0.992-0.996), effectively ruling out right-skewed distance distribution bias; (2) Null testing demonstrating 27-29× signal degradation under data scrambling (p < 0.01, validated through Step 3.7 corrections); (3) Rigorous cross-validation with exceptional temporal stability (LODO CV < 0.2%) and excellent spatial robustness (LOSO CV < 1.3%); (4) Comprehensive bias characterization demonstrating TEP signals exceed realistic methodological artifacts by 16.2× with 6.5× correlation length separation and complete 10/10 validation criteria achievement; (5) Geometric control analysis showing maximum spurious correlations of R² = 0.007 (24-47× lower than LOSO-validated TEP signals), ruling out network geometry artifacts; (6) Zero-lag leakage immunity validated through robust metrics (Im{cohy}, PLI, wPLI) demonstrating no common-mode contamination; (7) Elevation stratification revealing monotonic trends from λ = 1,785 km (Q1: -81–79m) to 4,566 km (Q4: 379–713m), excluding geographic artifacts; (8) Multi-band frequency analysis across 13 independent bands (10-1500 µHz, 506M pairs for CODE) demonstrating broadband correlation structure (R² > 0.85 from 10-200 µHz, CV = 2.9% for CODE) with gravitational enhancement in tidal bands (λ = 4,677 km mean) but persistent post-tidal signals (30-40 µHz band shows R² = 0.946 mean, ranking among strongest bands across all centers), comprehensively excluding classical tidal contamination (enhancement ratio 1.58×, not >3× required for frequency-selective tidal dominance) while quantifying systematic instrumental contributions through control bands (R² = 0.618 mean at 1000-1500 µHz, establishing 1.5× signal-to-systematic discrimination); (9) Temporal analysis showing negative correlation between anisotropy ratios and Earth's orbital speed (r = -0.512 to -0.638, p < 0.002, validated through Step 3.7 corrections), consistent with motion through structured fields; (10) Geographic bias validation across 39.1 million station pair measurements showing excellent consistency (CV = 3.5-6.5%); (11) Ionospheric independence confirmed through real data validation using authentic space weather indices, showing weak correlations (r = 0.12-0.13, p > 0.29); (12) Diurnal and seasonal TEP modulation demonstrating systematic day-night variations (1.019-1.076× day > night coupling) and seasonal peak hour variations, indicating multi-timescale scalar field coupling. Standard GNSS processing systematically suppresses TEP signals through multiple mechanisms (detailed in Section 4.5), indicating observed correlations represent conservative lower bounds on true field coupling strength, with raw data access potentially revealing effects 2-10× larger.

These observations suggest GNSS networks may serve as sensitive detectors of fundamental field couplings to atomic transition frequencies. The multi-center consistency, systematic validation, and agreement with theoretical predictions warrant comprehensive investigation across global precision timing infrastructure to determine implications for theories of spacetime structure and tests of extended gravitational frameworks.
