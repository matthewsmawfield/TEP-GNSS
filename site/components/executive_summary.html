Executive Summary

Key Findings

Primary Finding: Analysis of 62.73 million station pair measurements reveals exponential correlation decay patterns with characteristic length λT = 3,330–4,549 km across three distinct analysis center solutions (CODE, IGS Combined, ESA Final).

Statistical Interpretation: Primary fit significance is not inferred from 62.7M independent pairs. The pair count provides geometric coverage; inferential degrees of freedom are governed by bin count (Neff ≈ 25–28), station covariance, and block-bootstrap structure.

This robust and statistically significant—at the distance-binned, block-bootstrap level, not as raw-pair significance—empirical phenomenon represents systematic distance-structured correlations in clock frequency residuals. Comprehensive multiband frequency analysis (10–3000 μHz) across three distinct analysis center solutions shows remarkable consistency in signal detection, with exponential models achieving high goodness-of-fit to distance-binned data (R² up to 0.970, pooled fit on distance-bin means; ~28 bins) across optimal frequency bands. This cross-solution validation represents a crucial milestone, ruling out software- and center-specific implementation artifacts through diverse processing methodologies (Bernese, NAPEOS, and the IGS combination algorithm). These patterns are consistent with—but do not yet constitute proof of—screened scalar-field frameworks via continuous Temporal Topology that predict broadband coupling suppressed at large distances.

The correlations demonstrate sensitivity to Earth's orbital motion, with center-specific statistics (per §3.3.1): CODE r = −0.701, p = 3.9×10⁻⁶; ESA Final r = −0.571, p = 4.2×10⁻⁴; IGS Combined r = −0.793, p = 2.2×10⁻⁸; planetary gravitational influences (6 Bonferroni-significant astronomical events detected with 3–6σ confidence), and systematic environmental dependencies including elevation and geomagnetic latitude effects. The 6 significant planetary detections represent outcomes from a systematic analysis of 8 astronomical event windows, with conservative Bonferroni correction applied across this complete set of tests. A detailed diurnal analysis (Local Solar Time) reveals systematic temporal variations exhibiting synchronized early morning coherence peaks and seasonal modulation showing maximum spring equinox effects. Gravitational energy hierarchy analysis reveals sophisticated coupling mechanisms: Earth's 433-day polar wobble (Chandler wobble) exhibits stronger detection signatures (R² = 0.377–0.471, |r| = 0.61–0.69) than expected from its mechanical energy scale (note: this energetic comparison is heuristic—the detection is in correlation space on binned means), potentially indicating Moon-Chandler gravitational field modulation that amplifies coupling through time-varying Earth-Moon gravitational geometry.

Validation Framework

Extensive validation provides evidence for signal authenticity through:

  • Cross-center validation: Three distinct analysis center solutions (CODE, IGS Combined, ESA Final) achieve nearly identical multiband patterns, with distance-binned fits reaching R² up to 0.970 (pooled fit on distance-bin means; ~28 bins) (ESA Final), 0.920 (CODE), and 0.966 (IGS Combined), demonstrating that the spatial decay is not an artifact of an individual processing software implementation
  • Frequency specificity analysis: Multiband analysis reveals systematic frequency-dependent patterns with enhanced signals at tidal frequencies, persistent post-tidal correlations (30–40 μHz: R² = 0.946, pooled fit on distance-bin means; mean across centers), and appropriately reduced control band performance (1000–1500 μHz: R² = 0.618, pooled fit on distance-bin means; mean across centers)
  • Statistical robustness: Distance-binned fits R² = 0.920–0.970 on ~28 bins vs. null mean 0.015–0.040 (ΔR² = 0.89–0.95; z = 15.8–31.9, all p $\lt$ 10⁻¹⁵; 24–61× signal-to-null ratios across 180 scrambling iterations), with comprehensive multiple comparison corrections across 388 statistical tests showing 52% FDR-BH survival (40% under ultra-conservative Bonferroni correction)
  • Rigorous uncertainty quantification: Dual bootstrap approaches provide comprehensive validation — (1) 5000-iteration block bootstrap with 71–73 % convergence validates correlation parameters without bias, yielding λ 95 % CI ranges CODE 1,198–5,918 km, ESA 2,532–3,984 km, IGS 3,197–4,871 km; (2) station-block bootstrap with 30 % removal confirms robustness to network topology.
  • Data integrity: Daily Kp indices (226 records) were used without up-sampling; data gaps are reported rather than filled.
  • Alternative analysis: Systematic investigation addresses classical tidal contamination and potential methodological artifacts. Ionospheric validation using real geomagnetic data shows weak correlations (Kp: r = 0.12-0.13, p > 0.29; autocorrelation-corrected per Section 2.3) but comprehensive solar activity assessment requires additional real daily data sources
  • Cross-validation: Leave-one-station-out and leave-one-day-out analyses suggest temporal and spatial robustness

Theoretical Implications

The observed patterns are consistent with the Temporal Equivalence Principle's prediction of gravitational field coupling to atomic clock transport. The primary empirical quantity is the covariance length λT = 3,330–4,549 km, which defines the terrestrial saturation anchor RT(M⊕) ≈ 4,150 km (ρT ≈ 20 g/cm³; Paper 6, UCD) within the ~1.0–4.8×103 km cross-product family (Paper 14, Step 3.5). Precision tests constrain the local shear response, which is pairwise-screened where the ambient time-gradient is steep (the $\mathcal{S}_\Sigma(\mathcal{E})$ gradient branch; weak-field: $g \gg g_t$); GNSS measures a distinct clock-covariance projection rather than a microscopic Compton wavelength. The diurnal analysis identifies synchronized daily and seasonal modulations consistent with dynamical proper-time structure; raw-RINEX/SPP consistency and combined multi-GNSS product tests are established in Papers 3 and 14, while raw carrier-phase analysis and separately processed per-constellation tests provide the next discriminating checks.

Together with the 25-year longitudinal recovery (Paper 2), the raw-RINEX pseudorange confirmation (Paper 3), and the multi-constellation MGEX replication (Paper 14), these findings establish a persistent, distance-structured covariance in global atomic clock networks consistent with the Temporal Equivalence Principle, with direct implications for precision time-transfer metrology and dynamical-time models. Raw carrier-phase processing and non-GNSS optical-fiber and two-way satellite time-transfer comparisons provide direct tests of the unsuppressed coupling amplitude across independent timing architectures.

Next-Stage Experimental Discriminators

The completed three-center, 25-year, raw-RINEX, and MGEX analyses define a concrete set of out-of-ecosystem discriminators:

  1. Independent replication by other research groups using different methodologies and analysis approaches
  2. Raw carrier-phase GNSS analysis (building on the raw-RINEX pseudorange/SPP validation completed in Paper 3) to quantify processing effects and reveal full signal characteristics
  3. Separately processed per-constellation testing across GLONASS, Galileo, and BeiDou (extending the combined MGEX multi-constellation confirmation in Paper 14) for technology-independent confirmation
  4. Systematic investigation of remaining alternative hypotheses using independent datasets and complementary approaches
  5. Extension to optical clocks for enhanced precision and different systematic dependencies

Conclusion

The convergence of multiple independent observational domains—spatial correlations, spectral characterization, Earth motion coupling, and gravitational correlations—combined with validation across software-diverse processing chains, establishes robust statistical patterns in global GNSS networks that warrant comprehensive investigation. These findings provide compelling empirical evidence for systematic distance-structured correlations in atomic clock networks, with cross-center, 25-year (Paper 2), raw-RINEX (Paper 3), and multi-constellation (Paper 14) validation demonstrating that the decay is an intrinsic feature of the clock-network observations rather than a pipeline artifact. These results support environment-dependent proper-time transport as predicted by the Temporal Equivalence Principle and define quantitative targets for raw carrier-phase and optical-link experiments.
